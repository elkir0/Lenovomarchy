## 1. Logs without UART: preloader/LK output, ram_console/pstore, DRAM retention, expdb/AEE/mrdump, framebuffer

### Takeaway
The stock MT6785 stack has no live USB log stream from the preloader or LK. It keeps three post-mortem log stores that a USB-only workflow can read: (1) the preloader and LK logs in DRAM through `log_store` (`/proc/pl_lk` on the vendor kernel, also copied to the `expdb` partition); (2) the kernel console in a pstore/ramoops region at a fixed DRAM address; (3) LK's KEDump, which copies that pstore region and the ram_console into `expdb` after an abnormal reset. A 2026 MT6768 postmarketOS port found every boot problem past the bootloader through this `expdb` path. LK also hands the kernel a live framebuffer (`atag,videolfb` in `/chosen`), which makes simple-framebuffer an on-screen console. Whether DRAM survives a reset untouched on this Lenovo is not proven. MediaTek treats it as conditional on "DDR reserve mode".

### Cited Findings

It records the boot phase (PL=1, LK=2, KERNEL=3, ANDROID=4, SUSPEND…) in **PMIC register 0xA0E**, which survives a warm reset — [log_store.c](https://github.com/JeremyWildsmith/lenovo-yt-j706f-kernel/blob/main/drivers/misc/mediatek/log_store/log_store.c), [log_store_kernel.h](https://github.com/JeremyWildsmith/lenovo-yt-j706f-kernel/blob/main/drivers/misc/mediatek/log_store/log_store_kernel.h).

- **DRAM retention.** [CODE] The TOPRGU has a `MTK_WDT_MODE_DDR_RESERVE` bit (0x80). - [CODE] On an abnormal boot, LK's KEDump logs "ddr reserve mode enabled/disabled/failed". - [OTHER-SOC: MT6768, 2026-09/10] Moto G06 postmarketOS port, "Debugging without UART": "On an abnormal reset, LK's `kedump` copies the whole pstore/ramoops region into the **`expdb`** partition". ### Inferences
- A mainline or own kernel on the YT-J706F can get a Tab-S2-style "last console after reboot" by declaring a `ramoops` node at the preloader's pstore window (reference: 0x4D010000, 0xE0000), or at the address the Lenovo LK actually reserves. A second, crash-proof channel exists in parallel: LK's KEDump copy in `expdb`, read after reboot from Linux (`dd` of the `expdb` partition) or from download mode with mtkclient (`mtk.py r expdb`).
- The SRAM structures (ram_console header at 0x11D000, log_store header at 0x11DF00) and PMIC register 0xA0E survive any warm reset. They are a small reliable channel for "how far did the last boot get". Reading them from mainline needs a small custom driver or `/dev/mem`, since the vendor drivers are not upstream.
- A clean hang that only ends in a watchdog bite may leave nothing in `expdb` (MT6768 report). As on the Tab S2, the test initramfs should therefore turn failures and timeouts into a panic, and the kernel needs `panic=` / `CONFIG_PANIC_TIMEOUT` set, so that every failure becomes an "abnormal boot" that LK records.
- An on-screen console is available from the first kernel line with simple-framebuffer on LK's buffer (video-mode panel), without any display driver. It gives humans a view and gives the host something to read through a screenshot of nothing, so it complements pstore rather than replacing it. The JeremyWildsmith port used the same idea on the vendor kernel (`CONFIG_VT` plus `CONFIG_FRAMEBUFFER_CONSOLE` on mtkfb).

### Gaps
- No source settles whether the Lenovo YT-J706F preloader/LK enables DDR reserve mode on a hardware-watchdog reset, or on a normal `reboot`. Nor whether the preloader's DRAM calibration or memory test touches 0x4D010000. Test in section 9.
- The exact pstore/ram_console addresses on the Lenovo build are not confirmed: Lenovo can relocate them, and the reference addresses come from a generic MT6785 tree.
- It is unconfirmed whether Lenovo's LK builds `FASTBOOT_DUMP_LOG` (`oem dump_pllk_log`), `MTK_MRDUMP_ENABLE` or KEDump (`read_kedump_config()` can disable it).
- The `fb_base` and pixel format of the YT-J706F LK framebuffer are unknown. They can be read from `/chosen/atag,videolfb-*` once any kernel boots.

## 2. Watchdog: TOPRGU, the mainline driver, state at kernel handover, behaviour after a reset, boot reason

### Takeaway
The TOPRGU watchdog at 0x10007000 is already **running when LK jumps to the kernel**: the reference preloader and LK both arm it at 10 s in hardware-reboot mode, and a timeout reboots "bypassing the power key", with no human needed. Mainline `mtk_wdt` detects a running watchdog and keeps it alive until userspace takes over. Upstream does not list `mediatek,mt6785-wdt`, but the MT6785 mainline fork adds it, and the generic `mediatek,mt6589-wdt` fallback covers the basic function. After a reset, LK appends `androidboot.bootreason=` (for example `wdt_hw`, `Watchdog`, `kernel_panic`, `usb`, `reboot_longkey`) to the next kernel's command line. That gives a "why did I reboot" signal that needs no driver.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The mainline MT6785 watchdog maps directly onto the Tab S2 "watchdog armed by the initramfs" block, with one twist: the hardware already runs from the preloader onward at about 10 s. The kernel must therefore probe `mtk_wdt` built-in (not as a late module) within about 10 s of LK's jump, or the board resets in a loop. The MT6768 "no more resets" note matches this.
- `WATCHDOG_HANDLE_BOOT_ENABLED=y` makes the kernel feed the watchdog until userspace opens `/dev/watchdog`. A test initramfs that wants a hung kernel to reset should therefore open `/dev/watchdog`, or set `watchdog.handle_boot=0` / `open_timeout`, so that a userspace hang also resets. Hard kernel hangs (IRQs off) reset either way. The mainline timeout cap is 31 s.
- For a mainline kernel, the cheapest boot-reason channel is reading `androidboot.bootreason=` from `/proc/cmdline`. On this project, read only that key (`grep -o 'androidboot.bootreason=[^ ]*' /proc/cmdline`): the owner's rule is never to print the full kernel command line, which carries a serial number on the Tab S2 and probably on MTK too.
- The expected behaviour after a watchdog reset is a normal boot of the active slot (not fastboot, not KPOC), with KEDump writing to `expdb` first. A watchdog reset alone therefore never strands the tablet in a state that needs a human.

### Gaps
- Whether Lenovo's LK shows an AEE or "kernel panic" screen, or stops for a key, after a KE or HWT reboot. The reference code continues booting, but the display path (`aee` with `video_printf`) was not traced.
- Whether Lenovo changed the 10 s preloader/LK timeout or disabled the watchdog (`apwdt_en` env / `MTK_DOE_CONFIG_ENV_SUPPORT`).
- Whether the preloader clears `WDT_STATUS` before Linux runs (mainline `mtk_wdt` does not report bootstatus anyway).

## 3. Boot-mode selection from the host at every reboot: the preloader USB handshake

### Takeaway
Yes, in MediaTek's reference MT6785 preloader. On every boot where it detects a USB cable, the preloader enumerates as a CDC-ACM "MT65xx Preloader" device (VID 0x0E8D, PID 0x2000). It sends `READY` and accepts an 8-byte token during a 2.5 s handshake window: `FASTBOOT` (answer `TOOBTSAF`), `METAMETA`, `ADVEMETA`, `FACTFACT`, `FACTORYM`, `SWITCHMD`, `AT+NBOOT`. That is a full software replacement for Samsung's "reboot download", usable **at every reboot, including from a hung kernel after a watchdog reset**, as long as the cable stays plugged and the host listens. mtkclient, mtk-bootseq and mtkbootseq implement it, and one 2026 project does it from macOS. Two configurations can close the window: an SLA-enabled fuse forbids META commands only, while a "factory lock" skips the handshake entirely. Whether Lenovo's preloader keeps the window open is unverified.

### Cited Findings
- [CODE] Token table in the MT6785 reference preloader: `READY`, `METAMETA`→`ATEMATEM/ATEMATEX`, `ADVEMETA`→`ATEMEVDX`, `FACTFACT`→`TCAFTCAF`, `FACTORYM`→`MYROTCAF`, `SWITCHMD`→`DMHCTIWS`, `FASTBOOT`→`TOOBTSAF`, `METAFORB` (forbidden) and `DISCONNECT` — [meta.h](https://github.com/svoboda18/preloader/blob/main/platform/mt6785/src/core/inc/meta.h). `FASTBOOT` sets `g_boot_mode = FASTBOOT`, and `AT+NBOOT` forces `NORMAL_BOOT` with `BR_TOOL_BY_PASS_PWK` — [main.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/preloader/blob/main/platform/mt6785/src/core/main.c).
- [CODE] Timing: `CFG_USB_HANDSHAKE_TIMEOUT (2500) /* 2.5s */` and `CFG_USB_ENUM_TIMEOUT (8000) /* 8s */` unless the board's `cust_usb.h` overrides them — [platform.h](https://github.com/svoboda18/preloader/blob/main/platform/mt6785/src/drivers/inc/platform.h). `usb_handshake()` returns early with "PMIC not dectect usb cable!" when no VBUS is seen. While the tool is present, `usb_listen()` sends `READY` repeatedly — [handshake_usb.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/preloader/blob/main/platform/mt6785/src/core/handshake_usb.c). The defaults are `CFG_USB_TOOL_HANDSHAKE := 1` and `CFG_USB_DOWNLOAD := 1` — [default.mak](https://github.com/svoboda18/preloader/blob/main/platform/mt6785/default.mak).
- [CODE] Security gating in `bldr_handshake()`: if `seclib_sla_enabled()`, the handler gets `CMD_HNDL_ATTR_COM_FORBIDDEN` ("META DIS"), which the META, FACTORY and ATE branches check. **The `FASTBOOT` branch has no forbidden check.** With `MTK_FACTORY_LOCK_SUPPORT` and the lock set, "Tool connection is locked" and no USB handshake runs — [main.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/preloader/blob/main/platform/mt6785/src/core/main.c).
- [CODE] LK honours the preloader's choice: `meta_detection()` / `meta.c` sets `g_boot_mode = FASTBOOT` — [lk boot_mode.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/lk/blob/main/platform/mt6785/boot_mode.c), [lk platform/common/meta.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/lk/tree/main/platform/common). LK also enters fastboot on RTC `PDN1` bit 13 ("reboot to boot loader"), which the vendor kernel's `reboot bootloader` sets, and on a key at boot. `fastboot reboot-bootloader` is registered — [boot_mode.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/lk/blob/main/platform/mt6785/boot_mode.c), [sys_commands.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/lk/blob/main/app/mt_boot/sys_commands.c), [fastboot.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/lk/blob/main/app/mt_boot/fastboot.c).
- [CODE] mtkclient implements the same handshake. `META.Mode` holds `FASTBOOT`, `METAMETA`, `ADVEMETA`, `FACTFACT`, `FACTORYM` and `AT+NBOOT`. It waits for a device with `pid == 0x2000`, reads `READY` and writes the token. It also handles a vendor `METASLA` challenge, implemented for Infinix/Tecno only. CLI: `python mtk.py payload --metamode FASTBOOT` — [meta.py](https://github.com/bkerler/mtkclient/blob/main/mtkclient/Library/meta.py), [README-USAGE.md](https://github.com/bkerler/mtkclient/blob/main/README-USAGE.md) (HEAD of 2026-10-26).
- [CODE] mtk-bootseq (plugnburn gist, 2020-06-15, updated 2026-07-27) is a pyserial script: write the token, expect `"READY"+token[:-4:-1]` (so `READYTOO` for FASTBOOT). Its example port is `/dev/tty.usbmodem14200`. "Supported commands depend on the device and its preloader" — [gist](https://gist.github.com/plugnburn/b3b0bcfd926c48ec5373bea84ce59337). The rompelhd/mtkbootseq repackaging (2026-06) is the same — [mtkbootseq](https://github.com/rompelhd/mtkbootseq).
- [CODE, OTHER-SOC: MT6739, 2026-08] macOS specifics from a TCL flip-phone toolkit: the preloader "publishes a USB CDC-ACM interface (0e8d:2000, "MT65xx Preloader") for roughly 2-3 seconds on every power-up". On macOS the port is `/dev/cu.usbmodem*` and "only exists inside that 2-3 second window", so the script polls `glob("/dev/cu.usbmodem*")`. Use `cu.*`, not `tty.*`: "the tty device blocks on open waiting for carrier detect, which a preloader never asserts" — [tcl-flip-macos-unlock tools/bootseq.py](https://github.com/jackharvest/tcl-flip-macos-unlock/blob/main/tools/bootseq.py).
- [CLAIM, 2022-10-06] Oxygen Forensic: "A virtual MediaTek COM port will be exposed in the system for one second" when a powered-off device is connected over USB. Without a handshake the device "goes back to charging mode". Some devices need volume keys held to reach preloader or BROM mode — [Forensic Focus](https://www.forensicfocus.com/news/support-of-mediatek-devices-in-oxygen-forensic-detective/).
- [CODE] With a Download Agent loaded (BROM or preloader DA mode), mtkclient can also shut down into fastboot: `ShutDownModes.FASTBOOT = 2` ("bootmode 0: shutdown 1: home screen, 2: fastboot") — [xflash_lib.py](https://github.com/bkerler/mtkclient/blob/main/mtkclient/Library/DA/xflash/xflash_lib.py).

### Inferences
- A host daemon on the Mac can watch for `0e8d:2000` and answer every enumeration. It sends `FASTBOOT` when a reflash is queued, and does nothing (normal boot) otherwise. That gives "Download mode from software" at every reboot, including the watchdog reboot of a hung test kernel, without any cooperation from the kernel under test. It is strictly better than the Tab S2's `reboot download`, which needed a working kernel.
- The window is short (2.5 s handshake, often seen as 1 to 3 s) and needs the USB link up to the host. The daemon must poll fast, and any USB hub in between must not delay enumeration. A pyusb/libusb implementation (as in mtkclient) avoids the race with the macOS CDC-ACM driver creating the `cu.*` node.
- Since FASTBOOT ignores the SLA flag in reference code, it is the token most likely to work on a locked-down Lenovo. METAMETA is the most likely to be refused (`METAFORB`).

### Gaps
- Not verified on a YT-J706F: whether the preloader enumerates as 0e8d:2000 at every boot, the actual window, whether Lenovo enabled factory lock or SLA, whether Lenovo's `cust_usb.h` changes the timeouts. No mtkclient issue or XDA report specific to the YT-J706F was found.
- Behaviour of the Lenovo LK on `FASTBOOT` from the preloader: reference code honours it, Lenovo's is unverified.
- Whether a mainline kernel can set RTC PDN1 bit 13 (MT6359 RTC through the PMIC wrapper) for an in-kernel `reboot bootloader`. No mainline reboot-mode driver for it was found. The host-side handshake makes this unnecessary.

## 4. Power control without a human: long-press reset, power-off charging (KPOC), VBUS cycling

### Takeaway
On MTK, plugging USB into a powered-off tablet powers it on: a "charger-in" boot reason `BR_USB`, after which the preloader runs and opens the USB handshake window. The host can then pick fastboot, or let LK continue into power-off charging (KPOC). KPOC itself can be turned off from fastboot (`fastboot oem off-mode-charge 0`), after which a USB plug-in boots the normal kernel. A host with a per-port-power hub (uhubctl) can therefore power on a shut-down tablet by cycling VBUS, without touching it. What still needs a human: a kernel hang with the watchdog disabled or not armed, and a dead battery or deep-discharge lockout. Even then, only a power-key long press or a battery cut-off helps, not USB.

### Cited Findings
- [CODE] During USB enumeration the reference preloader checks every second; it powers off "when usb boot (ex. KPOC) but plug-out usb" (`!usb_accessory_in() && !pmic_detect_powerkey() && g_boot_reason == BR_USB` → `pl_power_off()`) — [handshake_usb.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/preloader/blob/main/platform/mt6785/src/core/handshake_usb.c).
- [CODE] KPOC decision in the MT6785 reference LK: with a charger detected (`upmu_is_chr_det()`) and no "force boot" (RTC alarm, META, **power key held, watchdog bypass reboot**, 2-second reboot), it enters `KERNEL_POWER_OFF_CHARGING_BOOT` if `off-mode-charge` is 1, else `NORMAL_BOOT`. The env variable defaults to 1 when absent. `fastboot oem off-mode-charge <0|1>` sets it, and `fastboot getvar off-mode-charge` shows it — [mt_kernel_power_off_charging.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/lk/blob/main/platform/mt6785/mt_kernel_power_off_charging.c), [sys_commands.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/lk/blob/main/app/mt_boot/sys_commands.c), [fastboot.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/lk/blob/main/app/mt_boot/fastboot.c). These commands exist when `MTK_KERNEL_POWER_OFF_CHARGING=yes` (which defines `MTK_OFF_MODE_CHARGE_SUPPORT`) — [rules.mk](https://github.com/svoboda18/lk/blob/main/platform/mt6785/rules.mk).
- [CLAIM, 2022-10-06] Connecting a powered-off device exposes the preloader COM port for about one second, then it "goes back to charging mode" — [Forensic Focus](https://www.forensicfocus.com/news/support-of-mediatek-devices-in-oxygen-forensic-detective/).
- [CODE] Long press: the Lenovo kernel's MTK keypad Kconfig documents `KPD_PMIC_LPRST_TD` "Long press reboot timeout period: 0->8s, 1->11s, 2->14s, 3->5s", active only with the `ONEKEY_/TWOKEY_REBOOT_NORMAL_MODE` options. The YT-J706F defconfig sets none of them, only `CONFIG_KPD_PWRKEY_USE_PMIC=y` — [keyboard/mediatek/Kconfig](https://github.com/JeremyWildsmith/lenovo-yt-j706f-kernel/blob/main/drivers/input/keyboard/mediatek/Kconfig), [yoga11_defconfig](https://github.com/JeremyWildsmith/lenovo-yt-j706f-kernel/blob/main/arch/arm64/configs/yoga11_defconfig). The PMIC is MT6359 (`CONFIG_MTK_PMIC_CHIP_MT6359=y`), with an MT6360 charger/PMU.
- [DOC] Mainline `mtk-pmic-keys` supports `mediatek,mt6359-keys`, with `power-off-time-sec` and `mediatek,long-press-mode` (0 disabled, 1 pwrkey, 2 pwrkey+homekey) — [mediatek,pmic-keys.yaml](https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/Documentation/devicetree/bindings/input/mediatek,pmic-keys.yaml). [CLAIM] A 2022 patch series describes the duration as a 2-bit `RST_DU` field (GENMASK(9, 8)) — [linux-mediatek, May 2022](https://lists.infradead.org/pipermail/linux-mediatek/2022-May/042137.html).
- [CODE] After a hardware watchdog reset, reboot needs no power key (`BYPASS_PWR_KEY`, "Time out reboot always by pass power key") — see section 2.
- [DOC/CLAIM] uhubctl supports macOS, including Apple Silicon, through Homebrew. Its README warns that some hubs "may still not cut off VBUS to port", and that "not many USB hubs correctly support per-port power switching". Its compatible list includes the Mac mini M4's two front ports — [uhubctl](https://github.com/mvp/uhubctl/) (summarised from search results, not fetched in full).

### Inferences
- State machine for an unattended host:
  1. Tablet running or hung with the watchdog armed: watchdog reset, normal boot (no KPOC: watchdog bypass), and the preloader window at each boot.
  2. Tablet powered off with the cable in: VBUS off then on through uhubctl, so a charger-in boot, the preloader window, then FASTBOOT or normal/KPOC.
  3. Tablet in KPOC: our kernel boots with `androidboot.mode=charger` and can ignore it (the Tab S2 project already ignores `androidboot.mode=charger`), or `off-mode-charge 0` avoids KPOC entirely.
- Human-only states: (a) a kernel or firmware hang with the watchdog stopped (for example a kernel that disables TOPRGU) and VBUS present: power-key long press of 5 to 14 s, depending on how the preloader/LK programs the PMIC; (b) a battery too low for the PMIC to boot (USB alone may keep it in a pre-charge loop); (c) a corrupted preloader or LK with no BROM fallback reachable without keys (section 8); (d) any Lenovo prompt (orange-state or unlock warnings that wait for a key).
- The VBUS trick only works from "off" (PMIC power-on on charger insertion), not from a hung "on" state. A hub whose per-port power really cuts VBUS is required. The Mac's own ports are not a substitute unless they are on uhubctl's list (only the Mac mini M4 front ports are cited).

### Gaps
- The YT-J706F long-press duration and mode (power alone, or power plus volume) are not documented anywhere found. Measure them.
- Whether Lenovo's LK keeps `oem off-mode-charge`.
- Behaviour with a fully discharged 7500 mAh battery (pre-charge in preloader, display) is unknown.

## 5. USB networking and console from Linux (gadget controller, early console, macOS hosts)

### Takeaway
MT6785 uses the MediaTek **MTU3** dual-role controller (SSUSB at 0x11201000), on both the vendor kernel (`CONFIG_USB_MTU3_PLAT_PHONE`) and the mainline fork (`mediatek,mt6785-mtu3`, peripheral-capable). Configfs gadgets with ECM, NCM, ACM and RNDIS are already enabled in the pmOS MT6785 kernel config. No USB "early console" exists on arm64: the only USB earlycon in Linux is xHCI DbC (`earlyprintk=xdbc`), documented for x86 hosts. USB logs therefore start only once MTU3 and the gadget are up, and pstore plus simplefb cover what comes earlier. On a Mac host, use CDC-ECM (the Tab S2 setup) or NCM; RNDIS is not supported natively.

### Cited Findings
- [CODE] Mainline fork: `ssusb: usb@11201000 { compatible = "mediatek,mt6785-mtu3", "mediatek,mtu3"; ... phys = <&u2port PHY_TYPE_USB2>, <&u3port PHY_TYPE_USB3>; ... }` with an `xhci` child. On Begonia: `maximum-speed = "high-speed"; usb-role-switch; vusb33-supply = <&mt6359_vusb_ldo_reg>`, wired to a `usb-c-connector` role switch — [mt6785.dtsi](https://gitlab.nura.eco/mt6785-mainline/linux/-/blob/7.1/arch/arm64/boot/dts/mediatek/mt6785.dtsi), [begonia-common.dtsi](https://gitlab.nura.eco/mt6785-mainline/linux/-/blob/7.1/arch/arm64/boot/dts/mediatek/mt6785-xiaomi-begonia-common.dtsi).

### Inferences
- The Tab S2's ECM+SSH design carries over unchanged once MTU3 probes in peripheral mode. MTU3 role switching needs either a forced peripheral role (`dr_mode = "peripheral"` or role-switch default) or a working Type-C/PD path (MT6360 on the Yoga). The Begonia DTS uses a `usb-c-connector` role switch.
- A CDC-ACM getty (`g_serial`/configfs ACM) next to ECM gives a console that works even when IP networking is broken. On macOS it appears as `/dev/cu.usbmodem*`, like the preloader port.
- netconsole over the ECM link could stream kernel messages once the gadget is configured. It requires enabling `CONFIG_NETCONSOLE`, which the pmOS config disables, and a configured host IP. It only helps after MTU3 is up, so it does not replace pstore for early crashes.

### Gaps
- YT-J706F Type-C wiring: role-switch source (MT6360 TCPC?) and whether MTU3 comes up as peripheral without PD negotiation. Not found in sources.
- No source on the YT-J706F's USB port speed (USB 2.0 or 3.x lanes).

## 6. Rescue layers: A/B slots, `fastboot boot`, BROM (replacing "Linux in BOOT, rescue in RECOVERY" and Download mode)

### Takeaway
The YT-J706F is an **A/B device with recovery-as-boot**, so there is no separate RECOVERY partition. The Tab S2 split (daily Linux in BOOT, rescue Linux in RECOVERY) maps instead to `boot_a` / `boot_b`, plus MediaTek's A/B retry counter. The preloader and LK decrement `tries_remaining` on each boot that never sets `successful_boot` and switch slot at zero. That gives an **automatic fallback to the known-good slot** after N failed test boots, which needs the watchdog to turn each failure into a reset. Unlocked MTK LK also supports `fastboot boot <img>` (RAM boot, nothing flashed) in the reference code. BROM (the mask-ROM USB mode, via mtkclient) is the last resort, but entering it normally needs keys or a crash.

Its SoC list includes `MEDIATEK_MT6785`, its boards include `xiaomi/board-begonia.c` (MT6785), and its only Lenovo board is `amar_row_lte` — [R0rt1z2/kaeru](https://github.com/R0rt1z2/kaeru) (updated 2026-11-14), [lib/Kconfig](https://github.com/R0rt1z2/kaeru/blob/main/lib/Kconfig), [soc/Kconfig](https://github.com/R0rt1z2/kaeru/blob/main/soc/Kconfig). [OTHER-SOC: MT6768] With kaeru, writing `boot-bootloader` into `misc` was the "reliable route to fastboot" — [pmos-moto-g06-lagos](https://github.com/NikuzoKO/pmos-moto-g06-lagos).
- [OTHER-SOC: MT6768] MTK LK quirks for a custom boot image: stock load addresses are required (otherwise LK "fails `mblock_reserve` and asserts in `mt_boot.c`"), "LK always decompresses the kernel", so it must be gzip, the cmdline needs `bootopt=64S3,32N2,64N2`, and on header v3/v4 LK expects the stock MTK `dt_table` blob — [pmos-moto-g06-lagos](https://github.com/NikuzoKO/pmos-moto-g06-lagos).

### Inferences
- Proposed mapping. The daily Linux lives in slot A, marked `successful_boot=1`. Test kernels are flashed into `boot_b` and that slot activated with `tries_remaining` set to 1 to 3 and `successful_boot=0`. A good test boot sets `successful_boot` from Linux (an abslot-tool-style write to `misc` at offset 2048). A failed test boot (panic or watchdog) burns tries until the preloader or LK switches back to A. That reproduces "test from RECOVERY, daily in BOOT" with an automatic return. This needs on-device confirmation, because only `boot` (not `lk`) is slotted on this tablet.
- `fastboot boot` (if Lenovo kept it) is even safer for kernel bring-up: nothing is written, a hang or watchdog reset returns to the flashed slot, and the host can re-catch the preloader window to try again.
- Fastboot entry without keys: preloader `FASTBOOT` token (section 3). Partition readback without fastboot support: mtkclient in preloader or BROM mode (`r expdb`, `r boot_b`) or kaeru `fastboot fetch`.

### Gaps
- Whether Lenovo's LK implements the A/B retry fallback as in the reference code (decrement in preloader and LK), and whether Linux must also clear any `misc` BCB. Whether `fastboot boot`, `oem off-mode-charge` and `set_active` are present: test with `fastboot getvar all` and `fastboot boot`.
- How to reach BROM on the YT-J706F without keys (crash path) or with keys (exact combination). No YT-J706F-specific report was found.

## 7. Precedents: people who ported Linux to MediaTek phones and tablets without serial; UART pads on the YT-J706F

### Takeaway
There is a direct precedent on this exact tablet: a 2026 postmarketOS port of the **vendor** kernel to the YT-J706F (JeremyWildsmith, August 2026). It debugged a post-splash bootloop down to an LCM name mismatch and turned on an on-screen framebuffer console. A mainline MT6785 tree also exists (postmarketOS, Redmi Note 8 Pro "begonia", kernel 6.16 then 7.1, MTU3, watchdog, UFS, display). Recent MTK ports without UART rely on `expdb`/KEDump pstore dumps, a USB telnet debug shell and deliberate panics (MT6768, 2026). Simplefb on LK's framebuffer is the standard first display (MT6765, MT8183). No public UART pad map for the YT-J706F was found.

The vendor-kernel route (JeremyWildsmith) works but needs the 2019-era vendor toolchain.
- If a UART is ever needed, reference code says the preloader logs on the first AP UART (0x11002000) at 921600 baud. Finding the pad needs the board open and a scope or logic analyser. It is an optional fallback, not part of the USB-only plan.

## 8. Tab S2 building blocks: what exists on MT6785/YT-J706F and what replaces the missing ones

### Takeaway
Every Tab S2 building block has a counterpart, and two are stronger on MTK: the boot mode is selectable from the host at every boot, even after a hang, and the slot fallback is automatic. The weak point is the same as on the Tab S2, whether DRAM logs survive a reset, and MTK adds `expdb` as a durable copy.

### Cited Findings
Mapping (sources as cited in sections 1–7):
- **Download mode from software (`reboot download`)** → preloader USB handshake token `FASTBOOT` (or no token for a normal boot) at every boot with a cable, 2.5 s window; plus `fastboot reboot-bootloader` and the vendor kernel's RTC PDN1 bit 13; plus mtkclient in preloader/BROM mode for raw partition I/O — [preloader main.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/preloader/blob/main/platform/mt6785/src/core/main.c), [mtkclient meta.py](https://github.com/bkerler/mtkclient/blob/main/mtkclient/Library/meta.py). Status: exists in reference code; Lenovo configuration unverified.
- **Linux in BOOT, rescue Linux in RECOVERY** → no RECOVERY partition (`BOARD_USES_RECOVERY_AS_BOOT := true`, A/B) — [BoardConfig.mk](https://github.com/JeremyWildsmith/android_device_lenovo_yoga11/blob/master/BoardConfig.mk). Replaced by `boot_a`/`boot_b` plus the A/B retry fallback — [bootctrl_api.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/lk/blob/main/platform/common/bootctrl/2.0/bootctrl_api.c), [abslot-tool](https://github.com/F-19-F/abslot-tool) — and `fastboot boot` RAM boots — [fastboot.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/lk/blob/main/app/mt_boot/fastboot.c).
- **Hardware watchdog armed by the initramfs** → TOPRGU already armed by the preloader and LK (10 s, reboot without power key). Mainline `mtk_wdt` (fork: `mediatek,mt6785-wdt`) takes over with `WDOG_HW_RUNNING` — [lk mtk_wdt.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/lk/blob/main/platform/mt6785/mtk_wdt.c), [mainline mtk_wdt.c](https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/tree/drivers/watchdog/mtk_wdt.c).
- **pstore/ramoops console read after reboot** → stock pstore window (reference 0x4D010000) plus LK KEDump copy to `expdb`, `/proc/pl_lk` for preloader and LK logs, and `androidboot.bootreason=` — [memory_layout.h](https://github.com/svoboda18/preloader/blob/main/platform/mt6785/src/core/inc/memory_layout.h), [KEDump.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/lk/blob/main/app/mt_boot/aee/KEDump.c), [mt_boot.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/lk/blob/main/app/mt_boot/mt_boot.c).
- **USB RNDIS/ECM networking with SSH** → MTU3 gadget with configfs ECM/NCM (both in the pmOS MT6785 config); ECM on the Mac as today — [pmaports config](https://gitlab.nura.eco/nura/pmaports/-/blob/main/device/testing/linux-postmarketos-mediatek-mt6785/config-postmarketos-mediatek-mt6785.aarch64).
- **Unattended-test knob (`t715.autotest`)** → no firmware equivalent; it stays a kernel cmdline or initramfs convention. The boot image cmdline is passed through LK (MTK LK appends its own `androidboot.*` keys, including `bootreason`) — [mt_boot.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/lk/blob/main/app/mt_boot/mt_boot.c).
- **Owner's rare forced restarts** → still needed only for a hang with the watchdog off, or a dead battery. Power-on from "off" can be done by VBUS cycling (uhubctl) because charger-in powers the board up — [handshake_usb.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/preloader/blob/main/platform/mt6785/src/core/handshake_usb.c), [mt_kernel_power_off_charging.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/lk/blob/main/platform/mt6785/mt_kernel_power_off_charging.c), [uhubctl](https://github.com/mvp/uhubctl/).
- **Added, with no Tab S2 equivalent**: an on-screen console from LK's live framebuffer (simplefb) — [atags.c](https://github.com/svoboda18/lk/blob/main/platform/mt6785/atags.c); BROM mask-ROM USB recovery through mtkclient — [mtkclient README](https://github.com/bkerler/mtkclient/blob/main/README.md).

### Inferences
- Proposed automation loop for the Mac:
  1. A daemon catches 0e8d:2000 and sends `FASTBOOT`.
  2. `fastboot boot` (or flash `boot_b` and `set_active b`) with the test image.
  3. Wait for the ECM link and SSH.
  4. On success, the test marks itself successful or the host restores slot A. On failure, the watchdog or a panic resets the tablet; KEDump writes `expdb`; A/B retries burn down, or the host re-catches the preloader window.
  5. The host pulls `expdb` and pstore and reads `androidboot.bootreason`.
  6. If the tablet is found powered off, cycle VBUS.
- The main open risk is Lenovo-specific firmware configuration (SLA or factory lock on the preloader handshake, a stripped fastboot command set, disabled KEDump), not the SoC.

### Gaps
- All "exists" statements for the preloader and LK are based on MediaTek reference code, and only the kernel side is Lenovo's own GPL release. Every firmware item needs one on-device check (section 9).

## 9. What can only be settled with the tablet in hand, and the exact test for each

### Takeaway
About twelve short tests, most of them on stock firmware before any unlock, settle every open firmware question. Run them in the order below. None needs UART.

### Cited Findings
- The tests below exercise mechanisms cited in sections 1–6. They are not findings themselves.

### Inferences
Proposed tests, in order:
1. **Preloader window and tokens (stock, locked).** On the Mac, run a 0e8d:2000 watcher: `python mtk.py payload --metamode FASTBOOT`, or the TCL `bootseq.py` adapted to `/dev/cu.usbmodem*`. Plug the cable into the powered-off tablet, then repeat with `adb reboot`. Pass: `READY` received, `TOOBTSAF` or `READYTOO` acknowledgement, tablet lands in fastboot. Record the window length (timestamps of enumeration and disconnect). Repeat with `METAMETA` to see whether SLA forbids it (`METAFORB`). Fail mode to note: no enumeration at all, which suggests factory lock or Lenovo disabling the handshake.
2. **LK command set.** In fastboot: `fastboot getvar all` (record `slot-count`, `current-slot`, `has-slot:boot`, `has-slot:lk`, `off-mode-charge`, `unlocked`, `secure`) and `fastboot oem help` (if implemented). Try `fastboot oem dump_pllk_log` and `fastboot oem off-mode-charge 0`. Pass: the variables and commands answer.
3. **Unlock, then `fastboot boot`.** After `fastboot flashing unlock` (wipes data), `fastboot boot` a known-good test image, such as the stock boot image or a pmOS Begonia-style image with a YT-J706F DTB. Pass: it boots without being written to flash.
4. **Stock log stores (before unlock, through adb; root may be needed).** Read `ls /sys/fs/pstore`, `cat /proc/last_kmsg | head`, `head -c 2000 /proc/pl_lk`, `ls /proc/device-tree/reserved-memory/`, `cat /proc/device-tree/reserved-memory/*/compatible`, and `xxd /proc/device-tree/chosen/ram_console`. This gives the real pstore and ram_console addresses on the Lenovo build. If adb lacks permission, read them from the first booted own kernel.
5. **DRAM retention across a watchdog reset.** From an own kernel with ramoops at the stock window, `echo c > /proc/sysrq-trigger` (panic → watchdog or `PANIC_TIMEOUT` reset). After reboot, check `/sys/fs/pstore/console-ramoops-0` and the `androidboot.bootreason=` key. Repeat with a hang that only the hardware watchdog ends (`echo 1 > /proc/sys/kernel/sysrq; echo l`, or a loop with IRQs off from a test module), and with a plain `reboot`. Pass: the previous console survives in each case. Also dump `expdb` (`mtk.py r expdb expdb.bin`, or `dd` from Linux) and look for `SYS_PSTORE_RAW`.
6. **Watchdog state at handover.** Boot a kernel without the `mtk_wdt` driver and time the reset after LK's jump: expected about 10 s per reference code. Then boot with built-in `mtk_wdt` and check `cat /sys/class/watchdog/watchdog0/state`, `.../timeout` and `.../bootstatus`.
7. **Boot reason and stage registers.** After each kind of reset in test 5, record `grep -o 'androidboot.bootreason=[^ ]*' /proc/cmdline` (never print the whole cmdline: serial number). With `/dev/mem` or `devmem`, read TOPRGU `WDT_STATUS` (0x1000700C) and `NONRST_REG2` (0x10007024) for the stage bits.
8. **A/B fallback.** Write a deliberately broken image into `boot_b`, `fastboot set_active b`, and set `tries_remaining` and `successful_boot=0` in `misc`@2048 (abslot-tool layout). Let it fail N times through watchdog resets. Pass: the tablet comes back on slot A by itself. Then check what Linux must write to mark a slot successful.
9. **KPOC and VBUS power-on.** Power off, then cut and restore VBUS on a uhubctl-capable hub (check with a USB power meter that VBUS really drops). Pass: the tablet powers on (charging screen or our kernel with `androidboot.mode=charger`) and the preloader window appears. Repeat after `fastboot oem off-mode-charge 0`: it should boot normally.
10. **Long-press reset timing.** Hold power alone, then power plus volume, with a stopwatch, from a running and from a hung kernel. Record 5, 8, 11 or 14 s and whether the tablet restarts or stays off.
11. **Framebuffer handover.** From an own kernel, read `/proc/device-tree/chosen/atag,videolfb-*` (fb_base, vram, lcmname). Then boot with a `simple-framebuffer` node at that base, 1200x2000 or the panel's real mode, and try formats a8r8g8b8 and a8b8g8r8. Pass: the kernel console appears on screen.
12. **BROM entry.** Power off, hold vol up + power or vol down + power (mtkclient README), and connect. Check whether mtkclient sees BROM (0e8d:0003) and whether `mtk.py crash` from a preloader session reaches BROM without keys. This settles whether a corrupted LK or preloader is recoverable over USB alone.
13. **Optional UART.** Open the back cover, look for TX/RX pads near the SoC or debug connector, and confirm with a logic analyser at 921600 baud during power-on.

### Gaps
- Tests 1, 2, 4, 9 and 10 can run on stock firmware. Tests 3, 5–8 and 11 need an unlocked bootloader (data wipe). Test 12 carries a small brick risk if misused (BROM writes) and should stay read-only.
